# Overview

Netflix operates as a single operating segment, deriving its revenues primarily from monthly membership fees for streaming content, with advertising, consumer products, and live experiences contributing a non-material portion of total revenue (10-k.txt:1721, 10-k.txt:2073). The company’s core strategy is to grow globally within the parameters of its operating margin target, offering a range of pricing plans including an ad-supported subscription plan to meet diverse consumer needs (10-k.txt:1719). In the latest fiscal year, total revenues reached $45,183,036 thousand, representing a 16% increase from the prior year, while operating income grew 28% to $13,326,603 thousand, expanding the operating margin to 29.5% (10-k.txt:2058, 10-k.txt:2060, 10-k.txt:2061). Management has shifted its primary financial metrics to revenue and operating margin, discontinuing the reporting of membership numbers and average monthly revenue per paying membership (10-k.txt:2063). The business model relies on a combination of proprietary intellectual property, a global content delivery network known as Open Connect, and a culture focused on excellence to retain talent and members (10-k.txt:1730, 10-k.txt:2109, 10-k.txt:1735).

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

Netflix competes in an intensely competitive market for consumers' leisure time, facing rivals including linear television, other streaming providers, video gaming, and social media (10-k.txt:1725). The company’s competitive advantage is anchored in its ability to "win moments of truth" by offering compelling, differentiated content and a superior user interface (10-k.txt:1726). In the current quarter, revenue grew 12% year over year to $12.9 billion, with operating margin reaching 34.6%, up from 28.2% in the same quarter of the prior year (exhibit-99-1.txt:14). This margin expansion was driven by the timing of expenses and revenue upside (exhibit-99-1.txt:14). Growth is supported by healthy retention in every region, with churn improving year-over-year globally and in the U.S. (exhibit-99-1.txt:61). The company is expanding its third-party content offerings through new partnerships, such as the TF1 partnership in France and a licensing deal with Disney, which brings titles like the *Ice Age* films to its service (exhibit-99-1.txt:26, exhibit-99-1.txt:130). Additionally, Netflix is expanding its advertising tier to 15 new countries in the coming year, with the first tranche launching in March (exhibit-99-1.txt:153). The company’s audience is approaching one billion people, which management cites as a key asset for partnering with studios and networks (exhibit-99-1.txt:26).
