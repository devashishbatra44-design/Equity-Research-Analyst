# Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation operates a membership warehouse and e-commerce business model centered on high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover achieved through low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products. The company explicitly states that this model enables it to "operate profitably at significantly lower gross margins (net sales less merchandise costs) than most other retailers" (10-k.txt:724). A core component of this durability is the membership fee structure, which provides continuing revenue and reinforces loyalty; in fiscal year 2026, membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5,907 million (10-k.txt:1036). The company’s primary operating KPI is comparable sales, which management identifies as the "most important driver of our profitability" (10-k.txt:1010). For the 52 weeks ended August 30, 2026, total company comparable sales increased 8.4% on an adjusted basis, excluding the impacts of gasoline prices and foreign exchange (exhibit-99-1.txt:17). This organic growth was supported by a strong member retention rate, with the worldwide renewal rate at 89.8% and the U.S. and Canada renewal rate at 92.3% at the end of 2026 (10-k.txt:770). The company also reported net income of $9,226 million, or $20.76 per diluted share, for fiscal year 2026 (exhibit-99-1.txt:20).

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

Costco’s competitive position is characterized by its ability to maintain "pricing authority" and consistently provide competitive values, which management believes is a skill "repeatedly demonstrated over the long-term" (10-k.txt:1010). The company’s growth in fiscal year 2026 was driven by both organic comparable sales growth and expansion. Net sales increased 10.1% to $297,247 million, driven by an increase in comparable sales and sales at new warehouses (10-k.txt:1032). Expansion contributed to this growth through the opening of 28 new warehouses, including three relocations, for a total of 25 net new warehouses (10-k.txt:1030). The company operated 939 warehouses worldwide at August 30, 2026, up from 914 at August 31, 2025 (10-k.txt:721). 

A significant portion of Costco’s growth is driven by its digitally-enabled sales, which represented 11% of total net sales in 2026 (10-k.txt:754). The growth in this segment is substantial, with digitally-enabled comparable sales increasing 20.9% on an adjusted basis for the 52 weeks ended August 30, 2026 (exhibit-99-1.txt:18). This digital growth outpaces the total company comparable sales growth of 8.4% (exhibit-99-1.txt:17), indicating a strengthening competitive position in the e-commerce channel. The company also benefits from its gasoline business, which represented 11% of total net sales in 2026 (10-k.txt:751) and positively impacted net sales by $3,501 million, or 130 basis points, in fiscal year 2026 (10-k.txt:1034). 

Costco’s competitive advantage is further supported by its supply chain efficiencies, including cross-docking and direct purchasing from suppliers, which lower costs associated with traditional distribution channels (10-k.txt:725). The company’s limited SKU count, with less than 4,000 active SKUs per warehouse in its core business (10-k.txt:731), allows for high inventory turnover and volume purchasing power. The company’s international expansion is also a key growth driver, with Other International comparable sales increasing 9.8% on an adjusted basis for the 52 weeks ended August 30, 2026 (exhibit-99-1.txt:16).

# Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

Costco’s future growth is expected to be driven by continued warehouse expansion, particularly in international markets where the base is smaller and square footage growth rates are generally higher (10-k.txt:1017). The company opened two new warehouses in its Other International segment in fiscal year 2026 (10-k.txt:1030). Management notes that as the warehouse base grows, securing desirable sites becomes more difficult, making square footage growth a comparatively less substantial component of growth (10-k.txt:1017). This suggests that future growth will increasingly rely on comparable sales growth and digital penetration rather than new store openings.

A key catalyst for future profitability is the continued penetration of Executive memberships, which represented 42,300 thousand of total paid members in 2026 (10-k.txt:769). Executive members earn a 2% reward on qualified purchases, which reduces net sales but drives higher spending and loyalty (10-k.txt:769). The penetration of sales to Executive members represented approximately 75.2% of worldwide net sales in 2026 (10-k.txt:769), indicating a strong base for future fee revenue growth. The company also faces risks from tariffs, which management states are "more likely to adversely impact rather than improve our results" (10-k.txt:1015). The company’s strategy to mitigate this includes working with suppliers to share cost increases and adjusting merchandise mix (10-k.txt:1011).

Strategic recommendations for investors should focus on monitoring the sustainability of comparable sales growth, particularly in the U.S. market, which saw adjusted comparable sales growth of 8.2% for the 52 weeks ended August 30, 2026 (exhibit-99-1.txt:14). The company’s ability to maintain high renewal rates, currently at 89.8% worldwide (10-k.txt:770), is critical to the durability of its membership fee revenue. Additionally, the growth in digitally-enabled sales, which grew 20.9% on an adjusted basis (exhibit-99-1.txt:18), is a key indicator of the company’s ability to adapt to changing consumer preferences and compete with pure-play e-commerce retailers.

Insight: The 20.9% (exhibit-99-1.txt:18) growth in digitally-enabled comparable sales significantly outpaces the 8.4% (exhibit-99-1.txt:17) total company comparable sales growth, indicating that digital channels are a primary driver of organic revenue expansion and a key component of Costco's evolving competitive moat.
