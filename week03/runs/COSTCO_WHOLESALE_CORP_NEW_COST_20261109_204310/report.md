# Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation operates a membership warehouse model designed to generate high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover by offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products (10-k.txt:724). The company’s profitability is driven by increasing net sales, particularly comparable sales, which allows for the leverage of selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses (10-k.txt:1010). In fiscal year 2026, the company operated 939 warehouses worldwide (10-k.txt:721). Net sales increased 10% to $297,247 million, driven by an increase in comparable sales and sales at new warehouses (10-k.txt:1032). Membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5,907 million, driven by new member sign-ups, membership fee increases, and upgrades to Executive membership (10-k.txt:1036). Net income increased 14% to $9,226 million, or $20.76 per diluted share (10-k.txt:1044). The company’s business model relies on a membership format that reinforces loyalty and provides continuing fee revenue, with a worldwide renewal rate of 89.8% at the end of 2026 (10-k.txt:770).

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

**Business-Model Durability: 5**
The business model demonstrates clear durability through its ability to maintain high inventory turnover and low shrinkage via a controlled membership environment (10-k.txt:729). The company reports that it often sells inventory before it is required to pay for it, even while taking advantage of early payment discounts (10-k.txt:724). This working capital efficiency is supported by a cash flow from operations of $15,825 million in fiscal 2026 (exhibit-99-1.txt:113). The model’s reliance on a limited SKU count of less than 4,000 per warehouse allows for volume purchasing efficiencies that enable profitable operations at significantly lower gross margins than most other retailers (10-k.txt:731).

**Customer Retention / Primary Operating KPI: 5**
Customer retention is a primary strength, with a member renewal rate of 92.3% in the U.S. and Canada and 89.8% worldwide at the end of 2026 (10-k.txt:770). Paid members increased from 76,200 thousand in 2024 to 84,100 thousand in 2026 (10-k.txt:763). Executive members, who earn a 2% reward on qualified purchases, represented 42,300 thousand of total paid members in 2026, with their sales penetration reaching approximately 75.2% of worldwide net sales (10-k.txt:769). This high penetration of premium memberships indicates strong customer engagement and willingness to pay for enhanced value.

**Organic Growth: 5**
Organic growth is evidenced by comparable sales growth of 8.4% for the fiscal year 2026, excluding the impacts of gasoline prices and foreign exchange (exhibit-99-1.txt:17). Digitally-enabled comparable sales grew 20.9% for the fiscal year, indicating strong organic growth in the digital channel (exhibit-99-1.txt:18). The company attributes comparable sales growth to increasing shopping frequency from new and existing members and the amount they spend on each visit (10-k.txt:1010).

**Expansion-Driven Growth: 4**
Expansion contributed to net sales growth through the opening of 28 new warehouses, including three relocations, for a total of 25 net new warehouses in 2026 (10-k.txt:1030). However, management notes that as the warehouse base grows, square footage growth becomes a comparatively less substantial component of growth due to the difficulty of securing desirable sites (10-k.txt:1017). The company also notes that new warehouse openings in existing markets can lead to cannibalization of sales at existing warehouses (10-k.txt:1017).

**Competitive Advantage: 5**
Costco’s competitive advantage is supported by its "pricing authority," which is the perception among members of consistently providing the most competitive values (10-k.txt:1011). The company maintains direct relationships with many producers of brand-name merchandise and does not obtain a significant portion of merchandise from any one supplier (10-k.txt:755). The use of private-label merchandise where quality and member demand are high further strengthens this advantage (10-k.txt:755). The company’s ability to adapt to economic changes through adjustments to pricing and merchandise mix, including increasing the penetration of private-label items, supports its long-term competitive position (10-k.txt:1010).

**Catalyst Evidence: 4**
Catalysts include the continued expansion of the Executive membership program, which increased from 35,400 thousand in 2024 to 42,300 thousand in 2026 (10-k.txt:769). The company also highlights the growth of digitally-enabled sales, which represented 11% of total net sales in 2026 (10-k.txt:754). Additionally, the company is pursuing diversification in its supply chain and seeking to expand in-country production to mitigate risks such as tariffs (10-k.txt:755).

**Moat Risk: 3**
Risks to the moat include the impact of tariffs on merchandise costs, which are more likely to adversely impact results (10-k.txt:1015). The company also faces vigorous and widespread competition from global, national, and regional wholesalers and retailers, including those with e-commerce operations (10-k.txt:1010). Furthermore, the company notes that its worldwide renewal rate is adversely impacted by membership growth in certain international markets and a higher penetration of memberships sold online, which renew at a slightly lower rate on average (10-k.txt:1018).

# Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

The company expects that its rate of square footage growth will generally be higher in many of its international markets due to the smaller base in those markets (10-k.txt:1017). Management continues to focus on maintaining "pricing authority" by working with suppliers to share in absorbing cost increases and by adjusting merchandise mix (10-k.txt:1011). The company is also investing in its e-commerce operations, which ship merchandise through its depots and logistics operations, as well as through drop-ship arrangements (10-k.txt:728).

Insight: The 20.9% growth in digitally-enabled comparable sales (exhibit-99-1.txt:18) is a non-headline operating KPI that has the strategic consequence of expanding Costco's reach beyond the physical warehouse footprint, potentially mitigating the risk of cannibalization from new store openings (10-k.txt:1017) and supporting the long-term durability of the membership model by increasing shopping frequency (10-k.txt:1010).

# Investment Recommendation

**Recommendation:** Buy

**Deciding Number:** The worldwide member renewal rate of 89.8% (10-k.txt:770). This metric is the primary indicator of the durability of Costco’s membership model and the stickiness of its customer base. A sustained rate above 90% would confirm the strength of the competitive moat, while a decline below 88% would signal erosion in customer loyalty and pricing power, directly impacting the long-term viability of the high-margin membership fee revenue stream.

**Principal Catalyst:** The expansion of the Executive membership program, which increased from 35,400 thousand members in 2024 to 42,300 thousand members in 2026 (10-k.txt:769). This growth drives higher sales penetration (approximately 75.2% of worldwide net sales) (10-k.txt:769) and supports the 11% increase in membership fee revenue to $5,907 million (10-k.txt:1036), providing a recurring revenue cushion that supports the company’s valuation.
