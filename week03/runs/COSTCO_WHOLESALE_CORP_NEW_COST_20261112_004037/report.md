# Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation operates a membership warehouse model designed to generate high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover by offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products (10-k.txt:724). The company’s profitability is driven by increasing net sales, particularly comparable sales, and leveraging selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses as a percentage of sales (10-k.txt:1010). In fiscal year 2026, Costco operated 939 warehouses worldwide, an increase from 914 in 2025 and 890 in 2024 (10-k.txt:721). The company’s business model relies on a membership format that reinforces loyalty and provides continuing fee revenue, with paid members totaling 84,100,000 at the end of 2026 (10-k.txt:763). Membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5,907 million in 2026, driven by new member sign-ups, fee increases, and upgrades to Executive membership (10-k.txt:1036). The company maintains a competitive position through direct relationships with producers, cross-docking logistics efficiencies, and a limited SKU count of less than 4,000 active stock keeping units per warehouse (10-k.txt:731).

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

Costco’s business model demonstrates strong durability, evidenced by a worldwide member renewal rate of 89.8% at the end of 2026 (10-k.txt:770). In 2026, gross margin percentage decreased three basis points, but increased 10 basis points excluding the impact of gasoline price inflation (10-k.txt:1038). SG&A expenses as a percentage of net sales decreased 10 basis points, indicating continued operational leverage (10-k.txt:1040).

U.S. comparable sales grew 8.2% adjusted, while Other International comparable sales grew 9.8% adjusted (exhibit-99-1.txt:14, exhibit-99-1.txt:16). **Organic Growth**
Organic growth is robust, with net sales increasing 10% to $297,247 million in 2026 (10-k.txt:1032). This growth was driven by an increase in comparable sales and sales at new warehouses (10-k.txt:1032). The company opened 28 new warehouses, including three relocations, for a total of 25 net new warehouses in 2026 (10-k.txt:1030).

**Expansion-Driven Growth**
Expansion contributed to net sales growth through the addition of 25 net new warehouses in 2026 (10-k.txt:1030). Management notes that as the warehouse base grows, square footage growth becomes a comparatively less substantial component of growth due to the difficulty in securing desirable sites (10-k.txt:1017).

**Competitive Advantage**
Costco’s competitive advantage is supported by its "pricing authority" and the ability to consistently provide competitive values (10-k.txt:1011). The company’s direct relationships with many producers of brand-name merchandise and its pursuit of supply-chain diversification and in-country production strengthen its position (10-k.txt:755). The company’s low shrinkage rates, attributed to the membership format and controlled entrances/exits, further enhance its cost structure (10-k.txt:729).

**Catalyst Evidence**
Catalysts include the continued penetration of Executive memberships, which increased from 35,400,000 in 2024 to 42,300,000 in 2026 (10-k.txt:769). The growth in digitally-enabled sales, which represented 11% of total net sales in 2026, provides a catalyst for future margin expansion as digital operations scale (10-k.txt:754). The company’s strategy to increase the penetration of private-label items is also a catalyst for gross margin improvement (10-k.txt:1010).

**Moat Risk**
Moat risks include the impact of tariffs on merchandise costs, which are more likely to adversely impact results (10-k.txt:1015). Competition is described as vigorous and widespread, including from e-commerce operations (10-k.txt:1010). The company’s reliance on gasoline sales, which represented 11% of total net sales in 2026, introduces volatility in gross margin percentage due to fluctuations in gasoline prices (10-k.txt:751, 10-k.txt:1016).
