# Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation operates a membership warehouse model that generates profitability through high sales volumes, rapid inventory turnover, and significantly lower gross margins than typical retailers. The company’s primary operating KPI is comparable sales growth, which management identifies as the "most important driver of our profitability" (10-k.txt:1010). In the latest fiscal year, net sales increased 10.1% to $297.2 billion (exhibit-99-1.txt:10), driven by an 8.4% increase in total company comparable sales (exhibit-99-1.txt:17). The business model relies on a limited selection of less than 4,000 active SKUs per warehouse (10-k.txt:731) and a membership base that reached 84.1 million paid members at the end of the latest fiscal year (10-k.txt:763).

The company’s competitive position is reinforced by its "pricing authority" and the efficiency of its cross-docking distribution network, which allows Costco to sell inventory before paying for it (10-k.txt:724). Membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5.907 billion in the latest fiscal year (exhibit-99-1.txt:38), reflecting growth in paid members and upgrades to Executive memberships, which represented 42.3 million of total paid members (10-k.txt:769). The worldwide member renewal rate was 89.8% at the end of the latest fiscal year (10-k.txt:770), indicating strong customer retention.

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

Costco’s market positioning is defined by its ability to offer low prices on a limited selection of high-quality products, a strategy that has resulted in 939 warehouses worldwide (10-k.txt:721). The company’s growth outlook is driven by both organic comparable sales and new warehouse openings. In the latest fiscal year, Costco opened 28 new warehouses, resulting in 25 net new locations (10-k.txt:1030). Management notes that as the warehouse base grows, square footage growth becomes a "comparatively less substantial component of growth" (10-k.txt:1017), shifting the focus toward comparable sales.

The competitive environment is described as "vigorous and widespread" across global, national, and regional wholesalers and retailers (10-k.txt:1010). Costco’s response to competition includes adjustments to pricing and merchandise mix, including increasing the penetration of private-label items (10-k.txt:1010). The company’s gasoline business, which represented 11% of total net sales in the latest fiscal year (10-k.txt:751), serves as a traffic driver, though it generally has a lower gross margin percentage (10-k.txt:1016).
