# Overview

Costco operates membership warehouses and e-commerce sites. The company began operations in 1983. It operates in the U.S., Puerto Rico, Canada, Mexico, Japan, the U.K., Korea, Australia, Taiwan, China, Spain, France, Sweden, Iceland, and New Zealand. The company operated 939 warehouses worldwide at August 30, 2026. The company operates e-commerce sites in nine countries. The common stock trades on the NASDAQ Global Select Market under the symbol “COST.”

The business model relies on low prices and a limited product selection. The company offers "nationally-branded and private-label products in a wide range of categories." This approach produces "high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover." The company operates at "significantly lower gross margins (net sales less merchandise costs) than most other retailers." The company often sells inventory before it is required to pay for it.

The company buys merchandise directly from suppliers. It routes goods to cross-docking consolidation points or directly to warehouses. This process creates "freight volume and handling efficiencies." The average warehouse space is approximately 148,000 square feet. The company carries less than 4,000 active stock keeping units (SKUs) per warehouse. The company averages from 9,000 to 11,000 SKUs online.

The company offers merchandise in core categories, warehouse ancillary services, and other businesses. Core categories include Foods and Sundries, Non-Foods, and Fresh Foods. Warehouse ancillary services include gasoline, pharmacy, food court, optical, hearing aids, and tire installation. The company operated 774 gas stations at the end of 2026. The gasoline business represented 11% of total net sales in 2026. Digitally-enabled sales represented 11% of total net sales in 2026.

The membership format is integral to the business. The annual fee for memberships is $65 in the U.S. Paid members were 84,100,000 at the end of 2026. Total cardholders were 150,400,000 at the end of 2026. Executive members represented 42,300,000 of total paid members in 2026. The penetration of sales to Executive members represented approximately 75.2% of worldwide net sales in 2026. The member renewal rate was 92.3% in the U.S. and Canada and 89.8% worldwide at the end of 2026.

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

The company seeks to provide members with "a broad range of high-quality merchandise at prices we believe are consistently lower than elsewhere." The company aims to maintain a perception of "pricing authority." The company does not focus on maximizing prices in the short term. It seeks to provide "the most competitive values."

Competition is "vigorous and widespread." It includes "global, national, and regional wholesalers and retailers." Competition also includes those with e-commerce operations. The company believes it has been successful in adapting to changes. It adjusts pricing and merchandise mix. It increases the penetration of private-label items. It expands online offerings.

Net sales increased 10% to $297,247 million in 2026. This increase was driven by comparable sales and sales at new warehouses. Higher gasoline prices positively impacted net sales by $3,501 million. Changes in foreign currencies positively impacted net sales by $1,332 million. Membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5,907 million. This increase was driven by new member sign-ups, fee increases, and upgrades to Executive membership.

Gross margin percentage decreased three basis points in 2026. It increased 10 basis points excluding the impact of gasoline price inflation. SG&A expenses as a percentage of net sales decreased 10 basis points. It increased one basis point excluding the impact of gasoline price inflation. The effective tax rate in 2026 was 24.7%. Net income increased 14% to $9,226 million. This equates to $20.76 per diluted share.

Costco's September 2026 retail month provides an early FY2027 demand signal. Net sales were $30.02 billion, up 13.0% year over year; comparable sales were up 11.4%, or 7.6% excluding the effects of changes in gasoline prices and foreign exchange, and digitally enabled sales grew 19.0%. The five-week period included a later Labor Day, which Costco said increased September comparable sales by a little over 50 basis points. This is encouraging, but one retail month and a calendar shift do not establish a full-year growth rate. Comparable sales are not the same measure as GAAP net-sales growth. Source: [Costco September 2026 sales release](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2026/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-September-Sales-Results/default.aspx).

The company opened 28 new warehouses in 2026. This included three relocations. The total was 25 net new warehouses. The company opened 18 warehouses in the U.S., five in Canada, and two in Other International. The company expects square footage growth to be higher in international markets. This is due to the smaller base in those markets.

# Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

The company expects to continue opening new warehouses. The rate of square footage growth is generally higher in international markets. The company expects this to continue. The company seeks to expand in-country production. It pursues diversification in its supply chain.

Government actions relating to tariffs affect merchandise costs. The degree of exposure depends on the type of goods, rates imposed, and timing. Higher tariffs are more likely to adversely impact results. The company works with suppliers to share in absorbing cost increases. It may pass cost increases on to members. It may hold prices steady despite cost increases.

The company aims to increase the penetration of Executive memberships. It seeks to sustain high renewal rates. The worldwide renewal rate is adversely impacted by membership growth in certain international markets. It is also impacted by a higher penetration of memberships sold online. These memberships renew at a slightly lower rate on average.

The company believes its warehouses are among the most productive in the retail industry. It attributes this to employee commitment and efficiency. The company targets a ratio of at least 50% of its base being full-time employees. The retention rate for employees with at least one year of tenure was approximately 94% in the U.S. and Canada in 2026.

The filing does not establish specific future revenue targets or detailed strategic initiatives beyond the general statements regarding warehouse expansion, supply chain diversification, and membership growth.

# Investment Recommendation

**Recommendation: Hold**

**Thesis**
Costco delivered strong operational results for the fiscal year ended August 30, 2026. Net sales increased 10% to $297,247 million. Net income increased 14% to $9,226 million. The company reported a worldwide member renewal rate of 89.8%. Its September 2026 retail-month release also showed 7.6% comparable-sales growth excluding the effects of changes in gasoline prices and foreign exchange, although the five-week period included a favorable Labor Day shift. Its FY-EPS P/E of 47.79 was above the peer set's TTM GAAP diluted-EPS mean of 27.90, a 71.31% premium on the supplemental comparison. This is a relative-valuation signal, not proof that Costco is overvalued: the peer periods end between July 31 and August 1, 2026, versus Costco's August 30, 2026. Target's TTM GAAP EPS includes a disclosed $994 million tariff refund. The analysis does not estimate intrinsic value or adjust for differences in growth, business mix, or risk. The current judgment is Hold: strong operating momentum is offset by a demanding valuation and insufficient evidence that the premium offers an adequate margin of safety. This is a current assessment, not a return forecast.

**Deciding Number**
The deciding number is the FY-EPS P/E of 47.79, calculated as the $992.13 October 27, 2026 close divided by FY2026 diluted EPS of $20.76. As a more recent cross-check, peer TTM GAAP diluted-EPS P/Es are 43.23 for Walmart, 15.37 for Target, and 25.09 for BJ's Wholesale; the simple mean is 27.90. Costco therefore trades at about a 71% premium to that mean. September's 7.6% adjusted comparable-sales growth supports the operating outlook, but it is a single five-week period and includes a favorable calendar shift; it does not establish the sustained earnings growth needed to underwrite the premium. This is the reason for Hold rather than Buy: the business has grown, but the comparison does not show that the current price offers an adequate margin of safety at that premium. It is not enough for Sell by itself: a relative premium does not establish overvaluation, and the reviewed evidence does not establish a deterioration that would justify a negative call. A Buy would need stronger evidence that future earnings growth supports the premium; a Sell would need stronger evidence that price exceeds defensible value or that the business outlook has weakened. Target's GAAP EPS includes a disclosed tariff refund, fiscal periods differ slightly, and no intrinsic-value estimate is provided, so the relative multiple is a caution signal, not a valuation conclusion.

**Limitation**
The base FY-EPS P/E comparison uses each company's latest annual diluted EPS, while the supplemental comparison uses latest available TTM EPS; the fiscal periods do not align perfectly. The sensitivity is scenario-based and does not estimate intrinsic value, include dividends, or control for growth, business mix, and risk differences. Treat all peer multiples as descriptive, not conclusive.
