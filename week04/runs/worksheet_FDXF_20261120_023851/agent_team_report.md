# Overview

FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc. (NYSE: FDXF) is the largest North American less-than-truckload (LTL) freight carrier, operating a network of over 365 locations, including approximately 355 shipping terminals and nearly 30,000 motorized vehicles across the United States, Canada, and Mexico (10-k.txt:739). The company completed its spin-off from FedEx Corporation on June 1, 2026, transitioning to a calendar-year fiscal reporting cycle effective for the period beginning June 1, 2026 (10-k.txt:752, 10-k.txt:763). The business model relies on a dual-service offering: FedEx Freight Priority, a premium time-definite service with a money-back guarantee, and FedEx Freight Economy, a value-oriented service for flexible transit requirements (10-k.txt:743, 10-k.txt:747).

For the four-month transition period ended September 30, 2026, the company reported revenue of $3.3 billion, a 9.2% increase year-over-year, driven primarily by favorable fuel surcharge revenue, higher weight per shipment, and an additional operating day, partially offset by softer volumes (exhibit-99-1.txt:41, exhibit-99-1.txt:75-85). The dual-service model of Priority and Economy allows the company to capture a broader range of customer needs, from time-critical shipments to cost-sensitive freight, enhancing its value proposition (10-k.txt:774-782).

The growth outlook is supported by the company’s focus on multi-faceted commercial initiatives, including streamlining online tools, improving customer support, and reducing cargo claims (10-k.txt:797). The introduction of a positive outlook for the three months ending December 31, 2026, with revenue expected to be $2.30 billion to $2.37 billion, compared to $2.07 billion in the prior year period, indicates management’s confidence in the base business (exhibit-99-1.txt:165-171).

This dynamic may limit the sustainability of revenue growth if pricing power erodes or if fuel prices decline. The company’s ability to manage its cost structure to match shifting volume levels will be critical to maintaining profitability (10-k.txt:1307).
