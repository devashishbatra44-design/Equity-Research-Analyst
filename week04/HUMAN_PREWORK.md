## Greenblatt Chapter 3

Who sells the spinoff, and why is that sale unrelated to value?

> Shareholders may sell shares in a newly distributed spin-off because they did not actively choose to invest in the newly independent company. Institutional investors may also sell if the spin-off is too small or does not meet their industry, index, liquidity, or investment-mandate requirements. Such selling may be unrelated to the company's underlying business value because it can result from portfolio constraints and the receipt of unwanted share distributions rather than a negative assessment of the company's operations.

## Klarman Chapter 10

When can the parent be cheap?

> The parent company may become undervalued when investors focus on the newly separated business and overlook the assets and earnings power retained by the parent. Transaction complexity, uncertainty surrounding liability allocation, and selling pressure from shareholders can further reduce investor attention to the parent. However, the parent should be considered undervalued only when the conservative estimated value of its retained businesses and assets, after accounting for debt and other obligations, materially exceeds its market capitalization. A low share price alone does not provide a margin of safety.
